Chapter 9: Ray Optics & Optical Instruments

Class 12 Physics — Comprehensive Study Notes & Worked Examples

1. Introduction & Nature of Light

Fundamental Concepts

  • Light: Electromagnetic radiation that produces the sensation of vision. In Ray Optics, light is treated as traveling along straight paths called light rays (Rectilinear Propagation of Light).
  • Ray of Light: A straight-line path along which light energy travels.
  • Beam of Light: A collection of light rays (parallel, convergent, or divergent).

2. Reflection of Light by Spherical Mirrors

Laws of Reflection

  • The angle of incidence ($i$) is equal to the angle of reflection ($r$): $i = r$.
  • The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal to the reflecting surface at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.

Sign Convention (New Cartesian Sign Convention)

  • All distances are measured from the Pole ($P$) of the mirror.
  • Distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive ($+$).
  • Distances measured opposite to the direction of incident light are negative ($-$).
  • Heights measured upwards perpendicular to the principal axis are positive ($+$); downwards are negative ($-$).

Important Terminology & Mirror Formulas

1. Relation between Focal Length ($f$) and Radius of Curvature ($R$):

$$f = \frac{R}{2}$$

2. Mirror Formula:

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$$

3. Linear Magnification ($m$):

$$m = \frac{h'}{h} = -\frac{v}{u}$$
  • $m < 0$ (negative): Real and inverted image
  • $m > 0$ (positive): Virtual and erect image

📝 Practice Questions

Question 1:

An object is placed at a distance of $30\text{ cm}$ in front of a concave mirror of focal length $20\text{ cm}$. Find the position, nature, and magnification of the image formed.

Solution:

Given (using sign convention): $u = -30\text{ cm}$, $f = -20\text{ cm}$

Using Mirror Formula: $\frac{1}{-20} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{-30} \implies \frac{1}{v} = -\frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{30} = -\frac{1}{60} \implies v = -60\text{ cm}$

Magnification: $m = -\frac{-60}{-30} = -2$

  • Image Position: $60\text{ cm}$ in front of the mirror.
  • Nature: Real, inverted, and enlarged ($|m| = 2$).
Question 2:

A convex mirror used for rear-view on an automobile has a radius of curvature of $3.00\text{ m}$. If a bus is located at $5.00\text{ m}$ from this mirror, find the position and magnification of the image.

Solution:

Given: $R = +3.00\text{ m} \implies f = +1.50\text{ m}$, $u = -5.00\text{ m}$

Using Mirror Formula: $\frac{1}{+1.50} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{-5.00} \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{1.50} + \frac{1}{5.00} = \frac{13}{15} \implies v = +1.15\text{ m}$

Magnification: $m = -\frac{1.15}{-5.00} = +0.23$

  • Image Position: $1.15\text{ m}$ behind the mirror.
  • Nature: Virtual, erect, and diminished ($m = +0.23$).

3. Refraction of Light

Refraction is the phenomenon of bending of a ray of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium into another due to a change in the speed of light.

Laws of Refraction

  • Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal all lie in the same plane.
  • Snell's Law: $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = {}^1\mu_2 = \frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}$

Refractive Index ($\mu$): $\mu = \frac{c}{v} = \frac{\lambda_{\text{air}}}{\lambda_{\text{medium}}}$

Real and Apparent Depth:

$$\text{Apparent Depth } (d') = \frac{d}{\mu}$$ $$\text{Apparent Shift } (\Delta d) = d \left(1 - \frac{1}{\mu}\right)$$

📝 Practice Questions

Question 1:

Light enters from air into a glass plate having a refractive index of $1.50$. What is the speed of light in glass? ($c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$)

Solution:

Using $\mu = \frac{c}{v} \implies v = \frac{c}{\mu} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{1.50} = 2 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$.

Question 2:

A tank is filled with water to a height of $12.5\text{ cm}$. The apparent depth of a needle lying at the bottom is measured to be $9.4\text{ cm}$. What is the refractive index of water? If replaced by a liquid of refractive index $1.63$, by what distance would the microscope have to be moved?

Solution:

$\mu_w = \frac{\text{Real depth}}{\text{Apparent depth}} = \frac{12.5}{9.4} \approx 1.33$

For liquid ($\mu_l = 1.63$): $d'' = \frac{12.5}{1.63} \approx 7.67\text{ cm}$

Shift distance = $9.4 - 7.67 = 1.73\text{ cm}$ downwards.

4. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

When light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle ($i_c$), the ray is completely reflected back into the denser medium.

Critical Angle Formula:

$$\sin(i_c) = \frac{1}{\mu}$$

Applications of TIR

  • Mirage: Optical illusion in deserts caused by thermal stratification of air.
  • Diamond Brilliance: High refractive index ($\mu \approx 2.42$) leads to a low critical angle ($i_c \approx 24.4^\circ$).
  • Optical Fibers: Light signal propagation via repeated TIR inside a high index core surrounded by cladding.

📝 Practice Questions

Question 1:

Calculate the critical angle for a glass-air interface if light velocity in glass is $2 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$.

Solution:

$\mu = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 10^8} = 1.5$

$\sin(i_c) = \frac{1}{1.5} = \frac{2}{3} \implies i_c = \sin^{-1}(0.667) \approx 41.8^\circ$

5. Refraction at Spherical Surfaces and by Lenses

Single Spherical Surface Refraction:

$$\frac{\mu_2}{v} - \frac{\mu_1}{u} = \frac{\mu_2 - \mu_1}{R}$$

Lens Maker's Formula:

$$\frac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$$

Thin Lens Formula & Power:

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}, \quad P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (in meters)}}$$

Combination of Thin Lenses:

$$\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2}, \quad P = P_1 + P_2, \quad m = m_1 \times m_2$$

📝 Practice Questions

Question:

A convex lens of focal length $20\text{ cm}$ is placed in contact with a concave lens of focal length $50\text{ cm}$. Find the power and focal length of the combination.

Solution:

$P_1 = \frac{1}{+0.20} = +5\text{ D}, \quad P_2 = \frac{1}{-0.50} = -2\text{ D}$

Total Power $P = +5 - 2 = +3\text{ D}$

Combined Focal Length $F = \frac{1}{+3}\text{ m} \approx +33.3\text{ cm}$

6. Refraction through a Prism

Key Relations for prism geometry:

  • $A = r_1 + r_2$
  • $i + e = A + \delta$

Prism Formula (Refractive Index):

$$\mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + \delta_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)}$$

For small-angle prisms ($A < 10^\circ$): $\delta = (\mu - 1)A$

7. Optical Instruments

1. Simple Microscope

  • Near point adjustment ($D=25\text{ cm}$): $m = 1 + \frac{D}{f}$
  • Normal adjustment ($\infty$): $m = \frac{D}{f}$

2. Compound Microscope

  • Near point adjustment: $m = -\frac{v_o}{u_o}\left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right)$
  • Normal adjustment: $m = -\frac{L}{f_o} \cdot \frac{D}{f_e}$

3. Astronomical Telescope

  • Normal adjustment ($\infty$): $m = -\frac{f_o}{f_e}$, Length $L = f_o + f_e$
  • Near point adjustment: $m = -\frac{f_o}{f_e}\left(1 + \frac{f_e}{D}\right)$
